# Meta Advertiser Guidance – Performance Surprise Framework

Meta hired me as a staff machine learning engineer to lead an advertiser-facing performance surprise initiative within the Advertiser Guidance team. The charter was to nudge advertisers inside Ads Manager with contextual cards, highlights, and guided remediation steps whenever we believed a campaign would deliver zero or near-zero results. The project sat at the intersection of UI experimentation, experimentation science, and machine learning reliability, so I was expected to translate ambiguous business goals into a buildable plan and to keep data science, frontend, and junior modeling contributors aligned.

I wrote the initial design docs for the performance surprise framework, capturing partner commitments, surface entry points, and the success criteria that would justify continued investment even though my overall tenure would be short. That framing helped de-risk the optics of a sub-one-year stint because leadership could point to a named program with a clear owner and company-level revenue metric.

On the technical side I hand engineered the data ingestion and preprocessing pipelines that normalized all features for the classifiers. Every upstream signal—campaign configuration, historical delivery, spend pacing, quality scores, and advertiser guardrail metadata—flowed through the same orchestrated Spark/Python jobs I owned, which meant I could audit and tune feature availability across both midflight and preflight contexts. I used those curated datasets to author the midflight model training code personally. The midflight classifier looks at campaigns that have been running roughly a week and uses their observed performance to predict a binary label: whether they are on track to produce zero results. I tuned the loss weighting, calibration steps, and thresholding strategy to limit false positives and shipped the experiment-ready model artifact. Once that pattern solidified, I paired with two junior modeling engineers so they could replicate the exact scaffolding for the preflight model, which only consumed signals that exist before a campaign launches (objective, budget, creative metadata, targeting templates, etc.).

Data scientists and I co-designed and monitored the production experiment. We launched guidance cards and highlights for six weeks behind a geo-sliced holdout. The classifiers predicted likely zero-result campaigns, the UI surfaced the recommendation to delete or rebuild that campaign, and we tracked incremental revenue as the north-star KPI. The lift cleared our pre-defined p-value threshold and accounted for roughly 0.2% of Meta’s total incremental revenue that year, making the program easy to defend despite the short timeframe it took me to stand it up.
